Each major cloud offers two kinds of AI service, and the names make it easy to mix them up. One kind is a **managed model API**: you call a model somebody else trained and hosts. The other is an **ML platform**: you bring data and code to train, tune and host models yourself.

For a full job-by-job translation of the names, start with [the cloud AI map](../cloud-ai-service-map/). This lesson is about choosing.

## The two families

<ServiceLayers client:visible />

The managed API side gives you hosted foundation models, with some reserved capacity on offer beside per-token billing. The platform side gives you notebooks, training jobs, endpoints and pipelines, billed for instance time, storage and any managed features, with full control of the model within your own compute.

<Callout title="Two recent renames" kind="note">
Microsoft announced at Ignite in November 2025 that Azure AI Foundry is now **Microsoft Foundry**. Google announced on 22 April 2026 that Vertex AI is now part of the **Gemini Enterprise Agent Platform**, and lists the renamed products on a name-changes page. Both old and new names still appear in docs and invoices. Search for both names.

## Questions that decide it

1. **Which models do you need?** Availability differs by cloud and by region, and some models are exclusive to one platform. Start with the models whose quality you have measured on your task.
2. **Where does your data live?** Data gravity matters. Moving data between clouds costs money, adds latency and often adds compliance questions.
3. **What do your compliance rules require?** Check residency, logging, private networking and what each provider says about training on your data.
4. **What do your teams already know?** Identity, networking and deployment pipelines you already run are a real advantage.
5. **Do you need to train or only to call?** If you need custom training, you need the ML platform side regardless.
6. **What will it cost at your volume?** Per-token versus reserved capacity switches over at some utilisation, which you can estimate with the cost calculator.
7. **How locked in will you be?** Keep prompts, evals and orchestration code portable so that you can move models when pricing or quality shifts.

## A practical stance

- **Pick the model first and the platform second**, unless a constraint such as data residency forces the platform.
- **Put a thin abstraction in your own code** around model calls: one function that takes messages and returns text plus usage. Switching platforms then touches one file, and your evals keep working.
- **Evaluate on your own cases before deciding.** Public leaderboards are a starting point, not a verdict.
- **Check capacity as well as features.** A model you cannot get throughput for is not an option. See [Quotas, capacity and regions](../quotas-capacity-and-regions/).
