## One token's journey

Every token starts as a vector (its embedding, from [Tokens and embeddings](../tokens-and-embeddings/)). That vector passes through a stack of identical blocks, often dozens. Each block reads it, mixes in information from other tokens, transforms it, and passes it on at the same size. Click each step.

## Where Q, K and V come from

Q, K and V are not extra inputs. The block holds three learned matrices, W_Q, W_K and W_V. Each token's current vector is multiplied by each of them to give its query, key and value. Because the matrices are learned, the model decides during training what "looking for" and "offering" mean at each layer. A fourth matrix, W_O, maps the attention result back to the model's width.

## Multi-head attention, and sharing K and V

Instead of one big attention, the vector is split into several **heads**, each with its own slice of Q, K and V. Each head computes its own weights, so one head can track the previous word while another links a pronoun to its noun. Their outputs are joined and mixed by W_O. Many small views beat one large average.

During generation, the keys and values of every earlier token are kept in the [KV cache](../../serving/kv-cache-and-memory/), and that cache often limits how many users fit on a GPU. Two variants shrink it:

| Variant | Query heads | Key and value heads | Cache size compared with standard |
|---|---|---|---|
| Multi-head (MHA) | H | H | 1x |
| Grouped-query (GQA) | H | G, each shared by H/G query heads | G/H |
| Multi-query (MQA) | H | 1 | 1/H |

With 32 query heads and 8 key-value heads, GQA keeps a quarter of the cache. The GQA paper found it gets close to standard quality with speed close to MQA, which is why many recent open models use it.

## Feed-forward, residuals and normalisation

The **feed-forward network** works on each token separately: expand the vector to a larger width (four times in the original paper), apply a nonlinearity, shrink it back. Attention moves information between tokens; the feed-forward part processes it within a token. Many current models use a gated variant (often called SwiGLU) with three matrices instead of two.

**Residual connections** add each sub-layer's output to its input. Each sub-layer only learns a correction, and training signals flow straight through dozens of layers.

**Normalisation** rescales the vector to a steady size so numbers neither explode nor vanish across the stack. The original paper normalised after the residual add (post-norm). Most current models normalise at the start of each sub-layer (**pre-norm**), which trains more stably. Many also replace LayerNorm with **RMSNorm**, which skips subtracting the mean and only divides by the root mean square: slightly cheaper, and it works as well in practice.

## Positional information

Attention on its own ignores word order: it is a weighted sum. Position must be added.

| Method | How | Seen in |
|---|---|---|
| Sinusoidal | Fixed sine and cosine patterns added to the embedding | The original Transformer |
| Learned absolute | A trained vector per position, added to the embedding | BERT, GPT-2 |
| Rotary (RoPE) | Q and K are rotated by an angle that depends on position, so their match depends on the distance between tokens | Llama and many other open models |

Learned absolute positions cannot go past the longest position seen in training. RoPE encodes relative distance directly, and context-extension methods work by rescaling its angles, though quality beyond the trained length still needs checking.

## Three shapes of transformer

| Shape | What it does | Suits | Examples |
|---|---|---|---|
| Decoder-only | Causal attention; predicts the next token | Chat, generation, code, general assistants | GPT series, Llama, Mistral |
| Encoder-only | Every token sees every other token; outputs a vector per token | Classification, embeddings for search, reranking | BERT and many embedding models |
| Encoder-decoder | Encoder reads the input fully; decoder generates while attending to it | Translation, summarisation, speech to text | The original Transformer, T5, Whisper |

## From the last layer to a token

After the final block and a last norm, one matrix (the **LM head**) turns the vector into one score per vocabulary token. **Softmax** turns scores into probabilities. Then a sampling rule picks:

- **Greedy**: always the most likely token. Repeatable, but can loop or sound flat.
- **Temperature**: divide the scores before softmax. Below 1 sharpens toward the top choice; above 1 flattens.
- **Top-k**: keep only the k most likely tokens, renormalise, sample.
- **Top-p (nucleus)**: keep the smallest set of tokens whose probabilities add up to at least p, renormalise, sample. If the top tokens have 0.5, 0.3 and 0.1 and p is 0.85, the first two only reach 0.8, so all three are kept and become about 0.56, 0.33 and 0.11.

## Mixture of experts

A **mixture-of-experts** (MoE) layer replaces the single feed-forward network with many expert networks and a small **router**. For each token, the router scores the experts and sends the token to the top few, then mixes their outputs by the router's weights. Switch Transformers used one expert per token; Mixtral 8x7B uses two of eight per layer, and its paper reports about 47B total parameters with about 13B active per token. The catch: compute follows active parameters, but memory must still hold all of them, and training needs an extra loss term to stop the router overloading a few experts. Serving often spreads experts across GPUs ([expert parallelism](../../infra/multi-gpu-parallelism-and-interconnect/)).

<Deep title="The equations">

Attention for one head, with d_k the head size and a causal mask on the scores:

```text
Attention(Q, K, V) = softmax(Q Kᵀ / √d_k) V
Q = X W_Q,   K = X W_K,   V = X W_V
MultiHead(X) = Concat(head_1, ..., head_H) W_O
```

A pre-norm block:

```text
h   = x + MultiHead(Norm(x))
out = h + FFN(Norm(h))
FFN(x) = W_2 · act(W_1 x)          # gated: W_2 · (act(W_1 x) ⊙ W_3 x)
```

LayerNorm and RMSNorm on a vector x of size d, with learned gain g (and bias b for LayerNorm):

```text
LayerNorm(x) = g ⊙ (x − mean(x)) / √(var(x) + ε) + b
RMSNorm(x)   = g ⊙ x / √(mean(x²) + ε)
```

Sinusoidal positions, for position pos and dimension pair i: `PE(pos, 2i) = sin(pos / 10000^(2i/d))` and `PE(pos, 2i+1) = cos(pos / 10000^(2i/d))`.

RoPE treats each pair of dimensions of q and k as a 2D point and rotates it by angle `pos × θ_i`, with `θ_i = 10000^(−2i/d)`. The dot product of a query rotated for position m with a key rotated for position n then depends only on m − n.

Parameter count for a dense block with width d and a 4d feed-forward: attention has 4d² (W_Q, W_K, W_V, W_O), the feed-forward has 8d², so about two thirds of the block is feed-forward. GQA shrinks W_K and W_V.

Sampling in plain Python:

```python
import math, random

def sample_next(logits, temperature=1.0, top_k=None, top_p=None):
    if temperature == 0:                                   # greedy
        return max(range(len(logits)), key=lambda i: logits[i])
    scaled = [l / temperature for l in logits]
    m = max(scaled)
    exps = [math.exp(s - m) for s in scaled]
    probs = [e / sum(exps) for e in exps]                  # softmax
    order = sorted(range(len(probs)), key=lambda i: probs[i], reverse=True)
    if top_k is not None:
        order = order[:top_k]                              # keep the k most likely
    if top_p is not None:
        kept, total = [], 0.0
        for i in order:                                    # smallest set reaching top_p
            kept.append(i)
            total += probs[i]
            if total >= top_p:
                break
        order = kept
    weights = [probs[i] for i in order]                    # random.choices renormalises
    return random.choices(order, weights=weights)[0]
```

The order of temperature, top-k and top-p differs between libraries, so check your inference engine's docs.

## What to take forward

- Q, K and V come from three learned matrices applied to every token's vector, split into heads.
- GQA and MQA share keys and values across query heads, cutting the KV cache by the ratio of query heads to KV heads.
- Each block is attention then feed-forward, each wrapped in a pre-norm and a residual add.
- Position comes from added vectors (sinusoidal, learned) or from rotating Q and K (RoPE).
- The LM head and softmax give probabilities; greedy, temperature, top-k and top-p decide the token.
- MoE adds parameters without adding much compute per token, but every expert still needs memory.
