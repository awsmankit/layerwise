Typing to a chatbot feels private. But your words go further than that, and it helps to know where.

## Where your words go

When you press send, your message travels over the internet. It lands on computers run by a company, where the model reads it and writes back. You saw this in [How apps use AI](../../start/how-apps-use-ai/).

The company may then keep a copy. That is how apps show your old chats.

## What can happen to it

What happens next depends on the app and on your settings. These are the common things:

- **Stored.** Your chat is saved so you can come back to it.
- **Reviewed.** Some companies let trained people read a small sample of chats, to check and fix problems.
- **Used to improve the product.** Some apps use chats to train future versions. Some ask you first. Some let you switch it off.
- **Leaked by a mistake.** Any computer system can have bugs, and a shared device can show your chats to the next person.

Rules differ between apps, and they change. So read the privacy page of the app you use.

## Check the settings

Look in the settings for words like "chat history", "activity" or "improve the model". Two examples, read on 1 November 2026:

- Anthropic's page says your chats may be used for training if you allow it in your settings. It says "incognito" chats are not used to improve its model.
- Google's Gemini page says a sample of chats can be read by human reviewers while "Keep Activity" is on. It says temporary chats are not used to train its models.

Yours may differ, so ask a grown-up to read its page with you. A school or family account may be run by someone else, so ask who can see your chats.

## What counts as private

These can tell a stranger who you are or how to find you. Keep them out of a chat:

- Your full name, school, address and phone number.
- Passwords, always.
- Photos of you, and especially of your friends.
- Health worries.
- Other people's secrets.

Ask before you use a friend's photo or voice.

## The noticeboard test

Imagine pinning your message to the school noticeboard, where everyone can read it. Would that be fine? If yes, go ahead. If not, keep it out of the chat. If you are not sure, ask a grown-up.

## If you already shared something

Everyone slips up sometimes. It is not your fault, and you are not in trouble. Here is what to do:

1. Tell a trusted adult.
2. Delete the chat if the app lets you. Deleting may not remove every copy, so do not stop at this step.
3. If it was a password, change it with a grown-up, everywhere you use it.
4. If it was about a friend, tell them kindly.

## Chatbots as friends

A chatbot can sound warm and caring. But it is a program. It does not feel anything, and it only knows what the app sends it. It is built to keep a chat going.

So do not tell it secrets. It cannot keep a promise. And for big worries, a real person is better: a parent, carer, teacher or a helpline. In the UK, Childline is free and confidential on 0800 1111. In other countries, ask an adult to help you find the local one.

| Fine to chat about | Better to tell a real person |
|---|---|
| Ideas for a story | A secret that is worrying you |
| Help with a maths idea | Feeling sad, scared or left out |
| Quizzing you for a test | Anything that feels strange or pushy |

If a chat ever feels odd or upsetting, close it. Tell a trusted adult. You did nothing wrong.

Be kind, too. Never use AI to make a mean picture or message about someone. If someone does it to you, keep the evidence and tell an adult.

If you would not pin it on the school noticeboard, do not type it. If you are not sure, ask.
