AI uses a few maths ideas again and again. Arithmetic and fractions are enough to meet them. Here each idea is a picture you can move.

## Arrows made of numbers

A **vector** is a list of numbers. You can draw it as an arrow. The list (3, 1) means: go 3 steps across and 1 step up.

AI turns almost everything into vectors. A word, a photo or a song becomes a long list of numbers. Things that are alike get arrows that point the same way.

## Do two arrows agree?

The **dot product** tells you how much two arrows agree. Multiply the across numbers, multiply the up numbers, then add. A big positive answer means they agree. Zero means they share nothing. A negative answer means they disagree.

Search tools use this idea to find text with a similar meaning. They ask: do these two texts point the same way? Drag the arrow tips below. Press "Right angle" and look at the answer.

## Matrices: machines for arrows

A **matrix** is a small table of numbers that works like a machine. Put an arrow in and a new arrow comes out. The machine can stretch, turn, flip or squash the whole grid at once.

A layer in a neural network is one of these machines. A big model has many of them. Training means tuning the numbers inside.

Open "A machine" below. Try "Turn 90 degrees", then "Squash flat". Why can you not undo the squash? Many different arrows land on the same spot.

## Slope: which way is downhill?

Picture a hill. The **slope** under your feet tells you which way is downhill and how steep it is. Flat ground has a slope of zero.

Training a model is like walking downhill in fog. The height is how wrong the model is. You cannot see the whole hill. You feel the slope, take a step downhill, and feel again.

Press "Step downhill". Then try the "Too big" step size. Then switch to "Bumpy hills" and start from the left.

## The chain rule: slopes multiply

Think of a toy car. Pressing the pedal 1 cm moves a gearbox 3 cm. Each centimetre of gearbox turns the wheel 2 cm. So the wheel moves 3 times 2, which is 6 cm.

The slopes along a chain multiply. That is the **chain rule**. A neural network is a long chain of steps. To learn which dial to nudge, training multiplies the slopes along the chain, one step at a time, going backwards.

## Probability is counting

Chance is counting. A die has six sides and one is a six, so a six comes up 1 time in 6.

Now a trickier case. A game has a cheat-detector. These numbers are made up. One player in 100 cheats. The detector catches 9 of every 10 cheats. It also wrongly flags 9 of every 100 honest players.

Imagine 1,000 players. Ten cheat and nine are caught. Of the 990 honest players, about 89 are flagged by mistake. So 98 players are flagged and only 9 of them cheat. A flag means a cheat only about 1 time in 11.

That is surprising, but true. Cheats are rare, so honest players fill most of the flags. Change the settings below. Counting like this is **Bayes' rule**.

<Deep title="For grown-ups: the same ideas with symbols">

Dot product: a · b = a₁b₁ + a₂b₂ + … It also equals |a||b|cos θ, so cos θ = (a · b) / (|a||b|). That is cosine similarity, used to compare embeddings. For (3, 1) and (1, 3): the dot product is 6, each length is √10, so the cosine is 6/10 = 0.6.

Matrix and vector: a 2 by 2 matrix M sends (x, y) to (m₁₁x + m₁₂y, m₂₁x + m₂₂y). The columns are where the two basic steps land. Chaining two machines is matrix multiplication, which needs the inner sizes to match: (m by n) times (n by p) gives (m by p). A neural-network layer computes Wx + b, then applies a non-linear function. The determinant is how much the machine scales area; zero means the grid collapses and the machine cannot be undone.

Slope: the derivative f′(x) is the limit of (f(x + h) − f(x)) / h as h shrinks to zero. For a function of many inputs, the gradient is the arrow of all the slopes. Gradient descent updates x to x − η f′(x), where η is the step size or learning rate. A step size that is too large overshoots, as you saw. Real loss surfaces have millions of dimensions, and what works in one dimension is only a guide.

Chain rule: if z depends on y and y depends on x, then dz/dx = (dz/dy)(dy/dx). Backpropagation applies this layer by layer. Multiplying many slopes smaller than 1 shrinks the result towards zero, and multiplying many larger than 1 makes it explode. These are the vanishing and exploding gradient problems. See [Backprop from scratch](../../deep-learning/backprop-from-scratch/).

Bayes' rule: P(cheat | flagged) = P(flagged | cheat) P(cheat) / P(flagged). Here that is (0.9 × 0.01) / (0.9 × 0.01 + 0.09 × 0.99) = 0.009 / 0.0981, about 9.2 percent, which matches 9 out of 98 above. Gigerenzer and Hoffrage (1995) reported that people reason about problems like this better when they are given counts of people rather than percentages. The grid above uses that form.

## What to remember

- A vector is a list of numbers, drawn as an arrow. The dot product says how much two arrows agree.
- A matrix is a machine that moves every arrow at once. Networks are made of them.
- The slope says which way is downhill. Training steps downhill, many times.
- Slopes along a chain multiply. That is the chain rule.
- Probability is counting. When something is rare, even a good detector gives many false alarms.
